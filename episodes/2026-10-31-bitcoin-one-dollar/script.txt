If Bitcoin fell to one dollar, the computers running it would earn about three dollars every ten minutes.
Nobody's predicting this. It's a thought experiment.
These computers, called miners, race to add a block of payments every ten minutes. The winner earns about three bitcoin: two hundred forty thousand dollars today.
At one dollar, that's three dollars, below the power bill. Most miners would quit.
The catch: each block is a puzzle. Fewer miners means slower blocks.
That happened in 2021: China banned mining. A third of mining power went dark. One block took over two hours.
The fix: every two weeks, the puzzle re-tunes. That time, it got twenty-eight percent easier.
Bitcoin would survive, but owners would lose. A firm called Strategy, the biggest corporate holder, paid sixty-four billion dollars for coins that would be worth under a million.
The lesson: Bitcoin's rules rescue the network, not the price, because no rule sets a floor. Critics call that the risk.
