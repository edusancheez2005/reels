Two thousand, eight hundred and sixty-one dollars a coin. Five minutes later, less than a tenth of a cent. And buyers couldn't sell.

This was the Squid Game coin, launched in twenty twenty-one by a team nobody could trace.

One buyer, John Lee, thirty, from Manila, put in a thousand dollars.

To sell, though, you needed a second coin: Marbles. Marbles came only from a game the team promised. That game never came out.

Three days before the crash, the price site CoinMarketCap warned buyers couldn't sell. People kept buying anyway.

The Marbles rule didn't stop the team. On November first, they cashed out an estimated three point three eight million dollars, and their website vanished.

John's thousand dollars was gone in minutes.

The buyers made two mistakes: ignoring a public warning, and trusting a team nobody could trace. Before buying a coin, check its trades for other people selling. A price means nothing if you can't sell at it.
