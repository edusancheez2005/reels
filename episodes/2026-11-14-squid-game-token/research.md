# The Squid Game token (SQUID), Oct–Nov 2021: buy, but never sell

Researched 14 Nov 2026 (scheduled run). Every URL below came from a web search this run. Topic from insights/plan.md (Sat 14 Nov),
told as a scam, not a meme-coin story (learnings: meme-coin framing flopped twice).

## Facts (each used on screen or in the voice)

1. **Peak $2,861 per coin, then < $0.001 within five minutes, on 1 Nov 2021.** CoinMarketCap Academy timeline (London time,
   1 Nov 2021): $38 at 6:00, $90 at 7:00, $181 at 8:00, $523 at 9:00, ~$2,861.80 at 9:35, **$0.0007926 at 9:40** (= less than a
   tenth of a cent). Gizmodo: "peaked at a price of $2,861 before plummeting to $0 around 5:40 a.m. ET" (= 9:40 GMT; BST ended
   31 Oct). Forkast: "just in five minutes, the price of SQUID crashed from $2,861 to zero." Cointelegraph: "crossing over $2,850
   at 09:35 UTC on Nov. 1", "$0.002 minutes later" (rounds differently; we use CMC's $2,861 / $0.0008, the majority figure).
   - https://coinmarketcap.com/academy/article/i-lost-everything-how-squid-game-token-collapsed
   - https://gizmodo.com/squid-game-cryptocurrency-scammers-make-off-with-2-1-m-1847972824
   - https://forkast.news/squid-game-crypto-rug-pull-investors-deadly-ending/
   - https://cointelegraph.com/markets/game-over-squid-game-inspired-crypto-scam-collapses-as-price-crashes-from-2-8k-to-zero
2. **Buyers couldn't sell.** Gizmodo: "no one who purchased the coin was able to sell." Cointelegraph (2 Nov): "traders had
   complained that they could not sell their SQUID holdings in the only available market, ... PancakeSwap."
3. **Launched October 2021** ("debut on Oct. 26", Cointelegraph; "launched on October 26", Entrepreneur; presale 20 Oct per
   Forkast). Named after the Netflix show; not affiliated with Netflix (Entrepreneur, PC Gamer). We say "October 2021" only.
   - https://www.entrepreneur.com/article/394810
   - https://www.pcgamer.com/uk/squid-game-cryptocurrency-scammers-vanish-with-dollar33m
4. **A team nobody could trace.** CMC Academy: the website named David Kanny as CEO, "but no LinkedIn profile exists. Searches for
   other named executives — Mabel Jah, Kevin Sam, Christian Abbigail, Daniel Jolia and Lawrence Dan — also drew blanks on Google."
   (screenshot: assets/cmc_team.png)
5. **John Lee, 30, Manila, put in $1,000; gone within five minutes.** NBC News (3 Nov 2021, "Investors call scam after collapse of
   new cryptocurrency Squid"): "John Lee couldn't believe his luck. The $1,000 investment he made in Squid ... had skyrocketed in
   price. But within five minutes Monday, his money disappeared." "'I watched Squid fall down in a matter of minutes,' Lee, 30, from
   Manila in the Philippines". (screenshots: assets/nbc.png headline, assets/nbc_lee.png)
   - https://www.nbcnews.com/tech/tech-news/squid-game-crypto-collapse-pushes-investors-cry-scam-rcna4399
6. **The trap: Marbles.** CMC Academy: "The other cryptocurrency is called Marbles — and this asset can only be earned by taking part
   in the project's play-to-earn game. Only those who have accrued Marbles actually have the ability to sell their SQUID on the open
   market." (assets/cmc_marbles.png). PC Gamer: "an 'anti-dump mechanism' was put in place that forced you to attain 'Marble' tokens
   via a play-to-earn game in order to sell." Entry fee 456 SQUID (CMC Academy; not used).
7. **The game never came out.** PC Gamer: the game had not been shown; it was to enter beta "sometime this month" (Nov 2021), and the
   site went dark on 1 Nov. Gizmodo: site "has disappeared, along with every other social media presence."
8. **Warning three days before the crash (29 Oct 2021).** Cointelegraph, 29 Oct 2021, "Users unable to sell Squid Game token clocking
   45,000% gains": CoinMarketCap's warning "We have received multiple reports that the users are not able to sell this token in
   PancakeSwap. Please exercise caution while trading." (assets/ct_oct29.png). 29 Oct → 1 Nov = 3 days.
   - https://cointelegraph.com/news/users-not-able-to-sell-squid-game-token-clocking-45-000-gains
9. **An estimated $3.38M taken.** Cointelegraph: "appears to be a $3.38-million 'rug pull'"; Gizmodo: "an estimated $3.38 million"
   (correction note: first estimated $2.1M); Forkast, Entrepreneur, PC Gamer: $3.38M. NBC: creators "appeared to cash out Squid
   tokens worth more than $3 million", per wallet data. Decrypt: "BNB tokens worth over $2 million" (one address). We say "an
   estimated $3.38 million" (majority figure, clearly labelled estimate).
   - https://decrypt.co/84999/squid-game-token-winner-scammer-made-2-million?amp=1
10. **Website vanished.** Gizmodo: SquidGame.cash "has disappeared"; PC Gamer: squidgame.cash "now defunct"; Forkast: website
    "now closed".

11. **People kept buying after the warning.** The price kept rising after the 29 Oct warning ($5.71 on 29 Oct per Cointelegraph;
    "over $38 on Sunday" 31 Oct, $90 on 1 Nov per Cointelegraph; CMC Academy: $38 at 6:00 → $2,861.80 at 9:35 on 1 Nov, volume ~$11M).
    A rising price on a market where only buying worked = people kept buying.
12. **The rule didn't stop the team.** Gizmodo on the rug pull: creators "cash out their coins for real money, draining the liquidity
    pool"; NBC: creators "appeared to cash out Squid tokens worth more than $3 million", per wallet data.

## The step for today (said aloud and shown)
Before buying a new token, look at its trades for real sells by other wallets (on a DEX's trade list or an explorer); a price you
can't sell at means nothing; "you can't sell yet" is the scam. (Grounded in facts 2, 6, 8.)
