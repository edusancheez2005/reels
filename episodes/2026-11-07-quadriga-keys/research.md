## Facts (each on-screen claim maps here)

1. **76,000 clients owed $215 million.** OSC staff report, Executive Summary: "Over 76,000 clients were owed a combined $215 million in assets."
   https://osc.ca/quadrigacxreport/index.html (screenshot assets/osc-owed.png). Global News (20 Jun 2019): "approximately 76,000 users a total of
   $74.1 million in cash and $140.5 million in cryptocurrency, for a total value of $214.6 million". https://globalnews.ca/news/5411203/quadrigacx-timeline-and-the-many-questions-still-to-be-answered/amp
2. **Gerald Cotten, co-founder and CEO of QuadrigaCX, a Canadian crypto asset trading platform.** OSC: "crypto asset trading platform QuadrigaCX
   (Quadriga) ... committed by Quadriga's co-founder and CEO Gerald Cotten"; Global News: "CEO of the Canadian cryptocurrency exchange QuadrigaCX".
   Clients "entrusted their assets to Quadriga" (OSC) = the exchange held their coins.
3. **Died in India, December 2018, aged 30.** Global News timeline: "Dec. 9, 2018: Cotten dies of complications from Crohn's disease in Jaipur, India,
   at the age of 30, according to Robertson." (assets/global-dec9.png). OSC: "On January 14, 2019, Quadriga announced that Cotten had died in India the
   previous month." CBC (https://www.cbc.ca/1.5607990): "30-year-old Cotten". (Cause of death not used on screen.)
4. **Widow: coins in a password-protected cold wallet on his laptop, she doesn't know the password.** Global News, Jan. 31 2019 entry: "Robertson says
   in an affidavit that she cannot access the company's assets because Cotten stored them in a password-protected "cold wallet" on his laptop. She says
   she does not know the password." (assets/global-laptop.png). Same entry: "there should be "in excess of $180 million in coins in cold storage.""
   "Cold wallet" = crypto kept offline (NPR: "cold wallets" ... "to securely store cryptocurrency offline").
5. **Everyone thought the passwords died with him / money lost because of his death.** OSC: "It has been widely speculated that the bulk of investor
   losses resulted from crypto assets becoming lost or inaccessible as a result of Cotten's death. In our assessment, this was not the case."
   (assets/osc-spec.png). Daily Beast (6 Mar 2019): "Cotten was the only person who had the codes to access the accounts".
   https://thedailybeast.com/auditors-say-crypto-wallets-drained-8-months-before-chief-died-with-passwords
6. **The exchange collapsed; court-appointed monitor Ernst & Young.** Global News: "Jan. 31: QuadrigaCX files for creditor protection"; "Jan. 28:
   QuadrigaCX halts its operations". NPR: "Quadriga has been under debt protection from its creditors since Feb. 5, by order of the Supreme Court of
   Nova Scotia. The court also appointed Ernst & Young to the role of monitor".
7. **Cold wallets empty since April 2018, eight months before he died.** NPR, 6 Mar 2019: "Six "cold wallets" Quadriga used to securely store
   cryptocurrency offline were expected to hold millions. But they were emptied out in April, months before Cotten's death was reported, "bringing the
   balances down to nil," audit firm Ernst & Young says." (assets/npr-six.png)
   https://www.npr.org/2019/03/06/700651500/crypto-mystery-quadrigas-wallets-are-empty-putting-fate-of-137-million-in-doubt
   CBC (amp.cbc.ca/lite/story/1.5040099): "The monitor said those accounts were emptied in April 2018". Daily Beast: "emptied eight months before
   Cotten's alleged death." April → 9 Dec 2018 ≈ 8 months. Global News also has a report: https://globalnews.ca/news/5014750/missing-cryptocurrency-cold-wallets/
8. **Fake accounts, fake balances, trading with customers' money.** OSC: "Cotten opened Quadriga accounts under aliases and credited himself with
   fictitious currency and crypto asset balances which he traded with unsuspecting Quadriga clients." "The bulk of the asset shortfall—approximately
   $115 million—arose from Cotten's fraudulent trading on the Quadriga platform." "Cotten covered this shortfall with other clients' deposits. In
   effect, this meant that Quadriga operated like a Ponzi scheme." "Cotten lost an additional $28 million while trading client assets on three
   external crypto asset trading platforms". (assets/osc-fraud.png). OSC report published June 2020 (CBC 1.5607990, Bloomberg 11 Jun 2020).
9. **Lost at least $169 million.** OSC: "The people who trusted Quadriga with their money and crypto assets collectively lost at least $169 million."
   (assets/osc-owed.png, osc-spec.png)
10. **Sole control, no checks, no proper books.** OSC: "What happened at Quadriga was an old-fashioned fraud wrapped in modern technology." "From 2016
    onwards, Cotten was in sole control of a company that had hundreds of thousands of clients" ... "He ran the business as he saw fit, with no proper
    system of internal oversight or controls or proper books and records." (assets/osc-sole.png)
11. **Customers couldn't see whether their coins were there.** OSC (same section): "Quadriga clients could not have known what Cotten was doing" ...
    "Clients had no means of verifying these claims or obtaining meaningful information about the handling of their assets."
12. Recovered: EY "was able to recover or identify just $46 million in assets to pay out to clients" (OSC). Not used in script (optional chip).
