# Two pizzas (skit, period) — research

Plan row: Sun 22 Nov · morning · crypto · skit · genre period (2010 as a costume drama) · bit understatement (plan said fast-forward; changed after script review). Researched 22 Nov 2026,
03:55–04:30 London. "You", the suit guy (eating the pizza) and Stonks (his royal food taster) are fiction; the dates, the forum post,
the trade and the dollar values are real.

## Facts (each with its source)

1. **The offer**: Bitcointalk, "Pizza for bitcoins?", post #1 by laszlo, "May 18, 2010, 12:35:20 AM": "I'll pay 10,000 bitcoins for
   a couple of pizzas.. like maybe 2 large ones so I have some left over for the next day." Captured today as
   assets/forum_post.png. https://bitcointalk.org/index.php?topic=137 (Script: "You asked a forum for two large pizzas, with some
   left over for tomorrow.")
2. **Where**: same thread, post #3 by laszlo (18 May 2010): "Jacksonville, Florida ... United States". Guinness World Records also
   gives Jacksonville, United States. (Script: "Florida.")
3. **The trade, 22 May 2010**: same thread, laszlo, "May 22, 2010, 07:17:26 PM": "I just want to report that I successfully traded
   10,000 bitcoins for pizza." ... "Thanks jercos!" (read via fetch; quoted also by CoinDesk, below).
   Guinness World Records, "First commercial Bitcoin transaction": 22 May 2010, Laszlo Hanyecz; a fellow user paid $25 to order two
   large pizzas and received 10,000 bitcoins, then worth around $41; Hanyecz "has said he does not regret the purchase".
   https://guinnessworldrecords.com/world-records/696240-first-commercial-bitcoin-transaction
   On-chain: txid a1075db55d416d3ca199f55b6084e2115b9345e16c5cf302fc80e9d5fbf5d48d, block 57,043, 22 May 2010, one 10,000 BTC output
   (third-party protocol guide: https://bortlesboat.github.io/bitcoin-protocol-guide/examples/pizza-tx.html). The block explorer
   (mempool.space) could not be fetched from this session (permission prompt unanswered), so the txid is not shown on screen and
   the voice relies on the forum post + Guinness + CoinDesk instead.
4. **Who delivered**: "made by Papa John's", bought by "a 19-year-old named Jeremy Sturdivant (username "jercos")" — CoinDesk,
   "What Is Bitcoin Pizza Day?" (16 May 2022, upd. 9 Apr 2024) https://www.coindesk.com/learn/what-is-bitcoin-pizza-day/ ; Fox
   Business (24 May 2021): Sturdivant (then 19) "filled the order, sending him two large pizzas ... from Papa John's."
   https://foxbusiness.com/markets/infamous-bitcoin-pizza-guy-who-squandered-365m-haul-has-no-regrets
   (Script: "Tonight, a nineteen-year-old takes the deal. Papa John's, to your door." Jercos isn't named: he's not the story.)
5. **Worth about $41 then**: CoinDesk: "he could get $41 for those bitcoins on a certain exchange website, which priced BTC at less
   than half a cent per coin." The same thread, post #4 by ender_x (18 May 2010): "you could sell those on
   https://www.bitcoinmarket.com/ for $41USD right now.." (assets/forum_41.png). Guinness: "around $41". (Fortune/Bloomberg 2018
   says "about $30": we use $41, the figure on the forum itself.)
6. **$690 million in 2021**: CoinDesk (same page): the bitcoins would be worth about $690 million at the all-time high "of $68,990"
   (10 Nov 2021). 10,000 × $68,990 = $689.9M. (Cut after review.)
7. **$1.26 billion in Oct 2025**: "Bitcoin reached an all-time high of $126,210 on October 6, 2025." (KuCoin News/Coinomedia,
   6 Oct 2026, "Bitcoin's $126,210 All-Time High Turns One Year Old": https://www.kucoin.com/news/flash/bitcoin-s-126-210-all-time-high-turns-one-year-old ;
   CoinMarketCap Academy: "Bitcoin Smashes $126K Record" https://coinmarketcap.com/academy/article/bitcoin-smashes-dollar126k-record-as-ethereum-rallies-to-dollar4700 ).
   10,000 × $126,210 = $1.262B. (Script: "In October twenty twenty-five, bitcoin hit a record. Those same coins: one point two six billion dollars.") Past prices with dates only;
   nothing about where it goes next, and no "today" value (not verified from a dated primary page this morning).
8. **Half his mined coins on pizza**: Hanyecz, quoted by Cointelegraph (26 May 2020): "I had mined a pool of about a hundred
   thousand [BTC] and I spent maybe half of it on pizza". https://cointelegraph.com/news/the-bitcoin-pizza-day-numbers-are-actually-worse-than-you-thought
   (Sources differ on the total; the script doesn't use it. It says "He'd earned those coins by helping run bitcoin" = he mined
   them himself: "I got pizza for contributing to an open-source project" (fact 9) and "I had mined a pool..." (this fact).)
9. **No regrets / "free pizza"**: Guinness ("has said he does not regret the purchase"); Cointelegraph quote: "With 100,000
   Bitcoins, I would be [a billionaire] right now. But then I wouldn't have gotten that pizza."; Bitcoin Magazine (Colin Harper,
   22 May 2019): "I wanted to do the pizza thing because to me it was free pizza," and "I got pizza for contributing to an open-source
   project." https://bitcoinmagazine.com/culture/the-man-behind-bitcoin-pizza-day-is-more-than-a-meme-hes-a-mining-pioneer
10. **First time bitcoin bought something real; Pizza Day**: Guinness: "First commercial Bitcoin transaction", 22 May 2010.
    CoinDesk explains 22 May is celebrated as Bitcoin Pizza Day. (Script ending.)

11. **The pizzas cost $25**: Guinness: a fellow user "paid $25" to order the two pizzas. **Four days**: offer posted 18 May 2010,
    trade reported 22 May 2010 (thread timestamps, facts 1 and 3). (Script: "Four days ago..."; "The pizzas cost twenty-five.
    You've overpaid." — $41 of coins for $25 of pizza.)

## Genre candidates
