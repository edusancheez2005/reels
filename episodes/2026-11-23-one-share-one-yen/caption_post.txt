In December 2005, a typo at a Tokyo broker offered 610,000 shares for 1 yen each. It was meant to be 1 share for 610,000 yen.

On 8 December 2005, Mizuho Securities entered the order backwards on a company's first day of trading. That was more than 40 times the shares the company had. A warning appeared and the order went through anyway. Mizuho tried to cancel several times, but a defect in the Tokyo Stock Exchange's system refused the cancellation. Buying the shares back cost about 40 billion yen (about $340 million then). The exchange's president resigned, and courts ordered the exchange to pay Mizuho about 10.7 billion yen.
