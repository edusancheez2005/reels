Two more wrong guesses, and seven thousand bitcoin are locked away forever. Stefan Thomas is a German-born programmer. In twenty eleven, a Bitcoin fan paid him seven thousand and two bitcoin for an explainer video. He kept the secret keys that move those coins on an IronKey, an encrypted USB drive. He wrote its password on paper, then lost the paper. After ten wrong guesses, the IronKey wipes itself for good. He's guessed wrong eight times. Those coins are now worth over half a billion dollars. So can't someone break in? In twenty twenty-three, security firm Unciphered offered to open his drive. Thomas said no. Another team was already on it. As far as anyone knows, the drive is still locked. His mistake: one password, on one piece of paper, with no second copy. Your wallet's backup is its recovery words. Write them down twice, in two different places. Lose one copy, the other still gets your coins back.
