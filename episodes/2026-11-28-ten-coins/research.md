## The real case: Satoshi Nakamoto → Hal Finney, 10 BTC, block 170, 12 Jan 2009

1. **The first bitcoin transaction took place on 12 January 2009: Satoshi Nakamoto sent 10 bitcoins to Hal Finney, recorded in
   block 170 (timestamp 2009-01-12 03:30:25 UTC).** "The transaction was a test to see if the client worked." Guinness World
   Records, "First Bitcoin transaction" (record page; WHO: Satoshi Nakamoto, Hal Finney; WHEN: 12 January 2009).
   https://guinnessworldrecords.com/world-records/696243-first-bitcoin-transaction — captured: assets/guinness.png
2. **Hal Finney, in his own words** ("Bitcoin and Me", bitcointalk, 19 March 2013, archived by the Satoshi Nakamoto Institute):
   "When Satoshi announced the first release of the software, I grabbed it right away. I think I was the first person besides
   Satoshi to run bitcoin. I mined block 70-something, and I was the recipient of the first bitcoin transaction, when Satoshi
   sent ten coins to me as a test. I carried on an email conversation with Satoshi over the next few days, mostly me reporting
   bugs and him fixing them." https://nakamotoinstitute.org/bitcoin-and-me/ — captured: assets/finney_para.png
3. **The fan** (same post): "I mined several blocks over the next days. But I turned it off because it made my computer run hot,
   and the fan noise bothered me. In retrospect, I wish I had kept it up longer, but on the other hand I was extraordinarily
   lucky to be there at the beginning." https://nakamotoinstitute.org/bitcoin-and-me/
4. **The mailing list was sceptical** (same post): "When Satoshi announced Bitcoin on the cryptography mailing list, he got a
   skeptical reception at best. Cryptographers have seen too many grand schemes by clueless noobs."
   Guinness: Nakamoto and Finney "were both active members of a cryptography-focused mailing list"; the transaction came "just
   three days after the first open-source Bitcoin client was released online."
5. **Nobody knows who Satoshi is**: Guinness calls him "Bitcoin's creator, known only by the pseudonym Satoshi Nakamoto" and lists
   WHERE: Unknown. (So "you've never met your buddy. Nobody has." is the true joke.)
6. **No price existed yet.** The first published dollar rate came on 5 October 2009 from New Liberty Standard: 1,309.03 bitcoins
   per US dollar, worked out from the electricity cost of running a computer for a year. Bitbo, "First Bitcoin Valuation"
   https://bitbo.io/calendar/first-valuation/ ; Bitcoin Magazine, "Bitcoin is up 6.5 billion percent since it was first priced
   in USD" https://bitcoinmagazine.com/culture/bitcoin-is-up-6-5-billion-percent-since-it-was-first-priced-in-usd
   (Bitbo notes the original forum post is gone; two independent outlets report the same figure. Voiced as "about thirteen
   hundred bitcoin" for a dollar, i.e. ten coins under a cent.)
7. **Who Hal was** (same post): worked on an early version of PGP with Phil Zimmermann, one of PGP Corporation's first hires;
   a Cypherpunk. Guinness: "an American software developer". Hal Finney died in 2014 (not shown; plan: warm, no illness on
   screen).
8. "Running bitcoin": Finney's post of 10 January 2009 (Cointelegraph, BeInCrypto). Not used in the voice (cut for length).

9. **Mining makes new coins**: miners add blocks to the blockchain and the protocol pays them newly created bitcoin (50 BTC a
   block in 2009). CoinDesk, "How Does Bitcoin Mining Work?" https://www.coindesk.com/learn/bitcoin-101/how-bitcoin-mining-works ;
   Hal's own "I mined block 70-something... I mined several blocks over the next days" (fact 2–3).

   with Satoshi over the next few days, mostly me reporting bugs and him fixing them" → turned mining off (hot, fan noise).
   "Transfer", not "payment": it paid for nothing (Guinness: "a test"); Bitcoin Pizza Day (22 May 2010) is the first purchase.
