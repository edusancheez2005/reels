## Facts (each used on screen or in the voice is marked ★)
1. ★ In September 2012 James Zhong took about 50,000 bitcoin from Silk Road, a dark-web marketplace, by tricking its withdrawal
   system; he pleaded guilty to wire fraud. [DOJ-1, DOJ-2: "approximately 50,000 Bitcoin"]
2. ★ How: about nine accounts, over 140 transactions in rapid succession. Example in the release: on 19 Sep 2012 he deposited 500
   bitcoin, and less than five seconds later made five withdrawals of 500 bitcoin, a net gain of 2,000. [DOJ-1, DOJ-2]
   → voice: "You put in five hundred, and take it out five times. In under five seconds."
3. ★ Where it was found: "in an underground floor safe; and on a single-board computer that was submerged under blankets in a
   popcorn tin stored in a bathroom closet." [DOJ-1, DOJ-2] (CNBC's agent says "an upstairs closet"; we use DOJ's wording.)
   → voice: "a popcorn tin. In the bathroom cupboard. Under some blankets." / "a floor safe, and a popcorn tin".
4. ★ The search: 9 Nov 2021, his house in Gainesville, Georgia, by search warrant; about 50,676.17851897 bitcoin seized, then
   worth over $3.36 billion; then the largest crypto seizure in DOJ history. [DOJ-1] → "November 2021", "50,676 bitcoin", "Georgia".
5. ★ The burglary: on the night of 13 March 2019 Zhong called 911 to report a burglary at his home in Athens, Georgia, and told
   police "hundreds of thousands of dollars in crypto currency" were stolen [CNBC]; the police incident report lists a briefcase
   with $400,000 and a USB thumb drive [CCN]. → "in 2019, someone breaks into your house... You call the police."
6. ★ The burglar was never caught: CNBC (Oct 2023): "The perpetrator remains at large." → "[suit] Any news on my burglar?"
   (a character's question; the voice does not state anything new about the burglar).
7. ★ How he was found: about six months after the 911 call (September 2019) a small amount, "around $800 worth", from the Silk
   Road coins was sent to an exchange account "registered in Zhong's name" [CNBC, citing Chainalysis]; the IRS then called the
   Athens-Clarke County police, whose burglary investigation "had been languishing" [CNBC]; the stolen items "raised red flags
   for the IRS" [CCN]. → "Six months later, you send eight hundred dollars of it to an exchange with your name on the account.
   ... Tax investigators follow it, and call the local police. Who already have your file."
8. ★ Every bitcoin payment is recorded on a public ledger anyone can read (general fact; DOJ-1 credits "state-of-the-art
   cryptocurrency tracing"). 
9. ★ Plea 4 Nov 2022 (one count of wire fraud, maximum 20 years) before Judge Paul G. Gardephe [DOJ-1]; sentence: one year and
   one day in prison, 14 Apr 2023 [DOJ-2, The Block]. → "The real thief, James Zhong, got a year and a day."
10. Also seized: $661,900 in cash, gold and silver bars, 25 Casascius physical coins; his 80% stake in a Memphis real-estate
    company [DOJ-1]. Not used.
11. "For six years, it works": Sep 2012 → Mar 2019 = six and a half years with no one on to him (CNBC: the IRS link came in
    Sep 2019). OK as "six years".
