## Facts used (every on-screen and spoken claim)

1. **On Monday 20 April 2020 the US oil price (WTI crude, the May 2020 futures contract on NYMEX) settled at −$37.63 a barrel.**
   - CFTC press release 8315-20, 23 Nov 2020: the May contract fell "to settle at -$37.63 per barrel on April 20, the day before the contract expired". https://www.cftc.gov/PressRoom/PressReleases/8315-20
   - CFTC Interim Staff Report (23 Nov 2020): "an all-time intraday trading low price of -$40.32 per barrel before a final settlement of -$37.63 per barrel." https://cftc.gov/media/5296/InterimStaffReportNYMEX_WTICrudeOil/download
   - Reuters (Stephanie Kelly, 20 Apr 2020, as republished by gCaptain): "The May U.S. WTI contract fell $55.90, or 306%, to settle at a discount of $37.63 a barrel after touching an all-time low of -$40.32 a barrel." https://gcaptain.com/oil-prices-crash-below-zero-for-first-time-in-history-amid-pandemic/
   → Script: "The day ends at minus thirty-seven dollars sixty-three."
2. **It was the first time below zero.** EIA, 27 Apr 2020: WTI front-month futures "were priced in negative dollars per barrel (b) for the first time since trading began in 1983." https://www.eia.gov/todayinenergy/detail.php?id=43495 · Reuters headline: "Oil Prices Crash Below Zero for First Time in History Amid Pandemic".
   → Script: "First time below zero." (of this contract's whole history, 1983 on; Reuters: "first time in history")
3. **About eighteen dollars the Friday before.** Reuters' fall of $55.90 to −$37.63 gives a Friday 17 April settlement of $18.27; the CFTC report gives the Sunday-evening open as "$17.73 per barrel".
   → Script: "Oil's eighteen dollars a barrel." (Friday 17 April; "you" buy then.) Tag: "$18".
4. **Zero just after 2 p.m., about minus forty twenty minutes later.** CFTC report: "between approximately 2:08 p.m. ET and the end of the daily settlement period at 2:30 p.m. ET, the May Contract traded at prices below $0 per barrel for the first time"; "the all-time low of -$40.32 at 2:29 p.m. ET." EIA: "At about 2:30 p.m. ET, WTI traded as low as -$40.32/b".
   → Script: "Just after two, it hits zero." / "Twenty minutes later: minus forty." (2:08 → 2:29 = 21 minutes; −$40.32) Tag: "−$40.32".
5. **One contract = 1,000 barrels of real oil, delivered at Cushing, Oklahoma.** CFTC report: "Each contract is for 1,000 barrels of physically-deliverable crude oil that meets specific standards set by NYMEX." "The WTI Contract is priced in U.S. dollars and Cushing, Oklahoma is the delivery point." EIA: "Market participants that hold WTI futures contracts through expiration must make or take physical delivery of WTI crude oil in Cushing, Oklahoma, unless they have made other arrangements ahead of time." The May contract expired the next day, 21 April (CFTC).
   → Script: "You promised to collect real oil, from one Oklahoma town." / "Tomorrow, you're locked in." (the May contract stopped trading on 21 April; whoever still held it had to take the oil during May) / "Never a bet. It was oil, with a delivery date." / "You buy a thousand" (barrels = one contract; three buys = "Three thousand barrels").
6. **The tanks there were nearly full.** EIA: "crude oil storage facilities at Cushing have 76 million barrels of working storage capacity"; inventories of 60 million barrels on 17 April, "implying that storage was 76% full", and "some of this physically unfilled storage may have already been leased or otherwise committed". CFTC report: "By March 2020, the working storage available at the Cushing facility was near capacity." Reuters: "Storage is filling quickly at Cushing in Oklahoma, which is where the crude is delivered."
   → Script: "Its tanks are nearly full."
7. **Why the price went negative: holders paid to get out.** EIA: "some contract holders resorting to selling their futures contracts at negative prices, in effect paying a counterparty to allow them to exit their positions." Reuters: "desperate traders paid to get rid of oil."
   → Script: "they pay people to take it" / "They pay whoever takes it off you." / "So people pay to escape." (holders who could not take delivery; EIA says only about 1% of futures go to physical delivery.)
8. **Why nobody was driving.** April 2020 lockdowns cut fuel demand (Reuters headline: "…Amid Pandemic"; EIA cites the demand fall).
   → Script: "Nobody's driving." (a figure of speech for collapsed demand, no number given)
9. **In real life small traders were closed out and owed their brokers money.** Bloomberg News, 8 May 2020, "Oil crash busted a broker's computers and inflicted huge losses": a customer "owed Interactive Brokers US$9 million"; another got "notice that he owed US$110,000". https://www.bnnbloomberg.ca/oil-crash-busted-a-broker-s-computers-and-inflicted-huge-losses-1.1433542 · CFTC release 8432-21 (28 Sep 2021): at that broker "negative prices were not displayed to customers"; "$1.75 million" penalty, "restitution of $82.57 million to its customers". https://www.cftc.gov/PressRoom/PressReleases/8432-21
   → Script: "In real life, you'd just get a bill." Nobody real is named or joked about; not on screen as a number.
10. Next day (not used in the voice): the May contract "settled at expiration on April 21 at a price of $10.01 per barrel" (CFTC). June settled at $20.43 on 20 April (Reuters).

## The fiction
"You", the friend, the pyjamas, the lorry and barrels arriving at a flat are made up, and the narrator says so ("This is a
cartoon"): real oil is collected in Cushing, and a small trader's broker closes the position first. Buying at exactly $0 is
the joke's version of the price crossing zero at about 2:08 p.m. No real person is named. Nothing tells the viewer to buy, sell
or hold; no price is predicted.
