Will Bitcoin go up, or down? Honest answer: nobody knows. But you can know where it came from, and what gives it value. October two thousand and eight. Banks are failing. Someone calling themselves Satoshi Nakamoto posts a nine-page paper: digital cash that moves between people, with no bank in the middle. January two thousand and nine, the first block. Hidden inside it, a newspaper headline: Chancellor on brink of second bailout for banks. The first ever transfer: ten bitcoin, to a programmer called Hal Finney. A year later, someone paid ten thousand bitcoin for two pizzas. Today, that's almost eight hundred million dollars. Then, in twenty eleven, Satoshi wrote that they'd moved on to other things, and vanished. An estimated million coins have never moved. Today, one bitcoin is worth about eighty thousand dollars, and American funds hold almost one point three million of them. So what gives it value? Scarcity: there will only ever be twenty-one million, and over twenty million already exist. No one can print more, or freeze yours. And millions of people agree it's worth something, like gold. Critics say that agreement is all there is. So, up or down? Nobody knows. Now you know what the bet is on.
