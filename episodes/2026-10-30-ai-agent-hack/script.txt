In just eleven days, one hacking group went after more than two hundred organisations in at least fourteen countries. And AI did most of the work.

That's according to CrowdStrike, a cybersecurity company. It says a China-linked group it calls Vapor Panda used a team of AI agents: AI that works by itself. They ran mostly on DeepSeek, a Chinese AI model.

So how did it work? One human gave the orders, in Chinese, and only stepped in for the big decisions. The agents did the rest. They tried several ways in at once. If one failed, they just switched to another.

The targets? Foreign ministries, defence and chip companies, and phone networks. CrowdStrike confirmed break-ins at nine organisations.

Here's the crazy part: speed. Human hackers type up to forty commands an hour. CrowdStrike says these agents could top fifteen hundred.

AI hacking is growing fast. Last November, the first known AI-run spy campaign went after about thirty targets. Now? Over two hundred.

The takeaway? AI can try ways in at machine speed. The boring basics, updates and two-step logins, shut the easy ways in.
