Google says a big enough quantum computer could crack a Bitcoin key in minutes. Glassnode counts about 6 million bitcoin exposed.
That machine doesn't exist yet: Google's paper says under 500,000 qubits, while its Willow chip has 105. Nobody knows when one will be built. It could only attack coins whose lock, the public key, is visible: Bitcoin's earliest coins, some newer address types, addresses reused after spending, or any coin while its spend waits to confirm. Glassnode counts 6.04M BTC like that, about 30% of all bitcoin. A draft upgrade, BIP-360, would keep locks covered; CoinShares says only about 10,200 BTC face real risk.
What you can do today: don't reuse an address. Let your wallet give you a fresh one each time, so your lock stays covered. (That covers the usual address types; Taproot addresses, starting bc1p, show the lock anyway, which is what BIP-360 would fix.)
