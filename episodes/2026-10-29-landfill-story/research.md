## Who and how
- James Howells: Welsh computer/IT engineer from Newport. [Certain] — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Howells
- 15 Feb 2009: first ran Bitcoin software at home in Newport; "in early 2009 he was able to mine 8,000 Bitcoin". These are his
  averred facts, assumed true for the strike-out application, so the script says "he says". [Certain as his claim] —
  Howells v Newport City Council [2025] EWHC 22 (Ch), para 15: https://caselaw.nationalarchives.gov.uk/ewhc/ch/2025/22
  (2013 press said 7,500: The Guardian, 27 Nov 2013.)
- The private key was in a wallet.dat file on his laptop's internal hard drive (2.5", black and silver, white label); "the
  only information that can enable access" to the coins. [Certain as his claim] — judgment paras 15–16 ("saved on an
  internal laptop hard drive"). He mined with a laptop [Certain] — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Howells ("started
  mining Bitcoin with a Dell XPS laptop"; Wired 2017, The Guardian 2013). No brand on screen.
- Bitcoin was nearly worthless in 2009: no exchanges or market; the price for Jan 2009–Mar 2010 is given as "basically
  nothing". [Certain] — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_bitcoin (value history table). Block reward then: 50 BTC.
- 2010: he removed the drive from the laptop and put it in a drawer in his home office (he still plugged it in by USB now
  and then for other files). [Certain as his claim] — judgment para 15.
- 4 Aug 2013: clearing out his home office, he found two hard drives in his drawers, the one with the key and a blank one;
  meant to throw out the blank one, "mistakenly picked up the Hard Drive and put it into one of the black bin-liners". He
  asked his partner to take the bags to the tip; the next morning (before 9 am) she had taken them to the council's landfill
  site. [Certain as his claim] — judgment para 15.
- Site: Docksway landfill, Newport (city's main waste facility; ~350,000 tonnes of waste). [Certain] — judgment paras 13–14.
- Nov 2013 headline: "Missing: hard drive containing Bitcoins worth £4m in Newport landfill site"; standfirst: 7,500 Bitcoins
  "that James Howells generated on his laptop" (The Guardian, Alex Hern, 27 Nov 2013). [Certain] —
  https://www.theguardian.com/technology/2013/nov/27/hard-drive-bitcoin-landfill-site

## What he tried, and why it failed
- Jan 2021: offered the council 25% of the proceeds to search; refused: the council said it "was not permitted to excavate
  the site" under its Natural Resources Wales permit. [Certain] — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Howells (CNN, 15 Jan 2021)
- Aug 2022: £10–11M search plan with AI, a mechanical arm, drones and robot dogs. [Certain] — Wikipedia (Telegraph, 2 Aug 2022; Fortune, 1 Aug 2022)
- Oct 2024: sued the council (£495M). [Certain] — Wikipedia (WalesOnline, 12 Oct 2024)
- The council's stated risks of excavating (health and safety of staff and residents, public health and environmental concerns,
  its statutory waste duties), quoted in the judgment; the site runs under a Natural Resources Wales permit. [Certain] — judgment paras 14, 54.
- 9 Jan 2025, HHJ Keyser KC: under s.14(6)(c) Control of Pollution Act 1974, "anything delivered to the authority by another
  person in the course of using the facilities shall belong to the authority"; "In my judgment, the defendant's argument is
  correct and provides a complete answer to the claim" (para 28); claim dismissed (para 55). [Certain] — judgment.
- Appeal dismissed March 2025 [Certain] — Wikipedia (BBC, 1 May 2025). Council said in Feb 2025 the landfill would close
  (BBC, 8 Feb 2025). Aug 2025 "given up on trying to acquire the drive" (Forbes Australia) — not used: the same outlet ran
  "a new plan to get it back", so the voice doesn't claim he gave up.

## The lesson (our advice, not a quote)
- One copy of the key = a single point of failure: lose the device, lose the coins (judgment para 16: the key is the only way in).
- The drive wasn't kept safe: it sat in a drawer with a blank drive for three years (2010–2013), and the wrong one went in
  the bin bag (para 15). Whether the two drives looked alike isn't known; the reel doesn't claim it.
- Fix: write down your wallet's recovery words (modern wallets give you 12 or 24), keep them in two separate safe places,
  and label the device that holds your key. Never throw away a drive without checking it.
