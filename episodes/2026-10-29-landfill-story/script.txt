In twenty thirteen, a man in Wales threw away a hard drive. The bitcoin it unlocks is worth about six hundred million dollars today.

He's James Howells, an IT engineer from Newport. In two thousand nine, when bitcoin was nearly worthless, his laptop earned him eight thousand of them, he says. Their only key sat on one hard drive.

Four years later, clearing out his office, he found two drives in a drawer: that one, and a blank one. He binned the wrong one. Next morning, it was in the city landfill.

He offered the council a quarter of the fortune to dig. They refused, citing health and environmental risks. He sued, and lost: by law, anything dumped there belongs to the council.

He made two mistakes. One copy of the key, so one lost drive meant losing everything. And he didn't keep that drive safe: it sat beside a blank one. Keep your wallet's recovery words in two safe places, and label the device that holds your key.
