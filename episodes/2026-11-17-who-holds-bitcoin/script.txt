Wall Street funds now hold more bitcoin than Satoshi, Bitcoin's inventor.
They're US funds sold like stocks, called ETFs. They hold about one point two five million bitcoin.
Satoshi is believed to hold one point one million, mined early and never moved.
Who else has a big pile?
Strategy, a software company turned bitcoin buyer, owns over eight hundred and fifty thousand.
Tracking firm Arkham puts the US government at about three hundred and twenty-five thousand, mostly seized from criminals.
Coinbase, an exchange where people buy crypto, beats Strategy. Arkham counts about nine hundred and sixty-nine thousand there.
The twist: most of those belong to Coinbase's customers.
Bitcoin moves with a secret code called a key. On an exchange, the exchange holds that key, and decides when your coins move.
Want your own pile? Use a wallet where only you hold the key. The wallet's backup words rebuild that key if your phone dies, so keep them on paper.
