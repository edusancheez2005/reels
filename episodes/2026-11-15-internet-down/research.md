## Facts (every on-screen claim)

1. **313 internet shutdowns in 52 countries in 2025**, a record (2024: 304; 2023: 289).
   "In 2025, Access Now and the #KeepItOn coalition documented 313 shutdowns in 52 countries, surpassing the appalling records from
   2024 (304) and 2023 (289)." / "Shutdowns reached a new record high in the past year".
   Source: https://www.accessnow.org/internet-shutdowns-2025/ (Access Now, published 31 Mar 2026). Access Now's shutdowns are
   intentional disruptions, hence "deliberately". Myanmar had a record 95 (same page).
2. **Your coins live on the blockchain, a shared record; the wallet only protects the keys.**
   bitcoin.org: "The blockchain is a shared public ledger that the entire Bitcoin network relies on. All confirmed transactions are
   recorded in it". https://bitcoin.org/en/how-it-works
   Ledger: "your crypto is not in your wallet itself, but on the blockchain." https://www.ledger.com/academy/crypto/whats-a-secret-recovery-phrase
3. **Blocks = batches of confirmed payments; miners confirm by packing them into blocks.**
   bitcoin.org: "Mining is a distributed consensus system that is used to confirm pending transactions by including them in the
   blockchain." / "To be confirmed, transactions must be packed into a block". "All transactions are broadcast to the network and
   usually receive their first confirmation within about 10 to 60 minutes, through a process called mining." (same URL)
4. **Blockstream Satellite launched 15 Aug 2017, broadcasting Bitcoin blocks from satellites, free.**
   "Blockstream Satellite is the world's first service that broadcasts real-time Bitcoin transactions and blocks from a group of
   satellites in space. With the service, everyone will have free access to the Bitcoin network, in any corner of the world,
   including the estimated four billion people not currently connected to the Internet". Byline Adam Back, Aug 15, 2017.
   https://blog.blockstream.com/en-announcing-blockstream-satellite/
5. **Still running in 2026; needs a dish.** Blockstream Help Center (© 2026, current setup guides): "Coverage comes from five
   satellites spanning the Americas, Europe, Africa, and Asia-Pacific" and "Five years ago today, we launched #BlockstreamSatellite
   and made terrestrial internet optional for #Bitcoin users." Setup: "Mount the low-noise block downconverter to the satellite
   dish." https://help.blockstream.com/blockstream-satellite (a video caption on the same page says 4 satellites/6 carriers: we
   don't put a satellite count on screen). 2.0 post (4 May 2020): "A minimum 60cm / 24in dish is required for full
   synchronization". https://blog.blockstream.com/en-announcing-blockstream-satellite-2/
   "Free": the 2017 post says free access; receiving costs only the equipment.
6. **You can sign a payment with no internet; it still has to be broadcast to count.**
   Coldcard: "Air-gapped signing is a method of authorizing Bitcoin transactions that keeps the private key on a device that never
   connects to an internet-connected computer or phone." / "Coordinator software on your internet-connected computer or phone builds
   and broadcasts the transaction." https://coldcard.com/learn/advanced-concepts/air-gap-signing-methods
   bitcoin.org (above): transactions are broadcast to the network and confirmed by mining.
7. **March 2019: Rodolfo Novak (co-founder of Coinkite) sent a bitcoin (Lightning) payment over ham radio from Toronto, Canada, to
   Elaine Ou (developer and Bloomberg columnist) in San Francisco.**
   CoinDesk, "Bitcoin Coders Send International Lightning Payment Over Ham Radio" (URL path dated 2019/03/04): "sent by Rodolfo
   Novak, co-founder of bitcoin hardware startup CoinKite, to developer and Bloomberg columnist Elaine Ou." / "moved real bitcoin
   from Toronto, Canada, to San Francisco, California."
   https://www.coindesk.com/markets/2019/03/04/bitcoin-coders-send-international-lightning-payment-over-ham-radio
   (read via https://hotfix.coindesk.com/markets/2019/03/04/bitcoin-coders-send-international-lightning-payment-over-ham-radio;
   the page is behind a browser checkpoint from this workspace → Quote card). "Boss of wallet maker Coinkite": Coinkite makes the
   COLDCARD hardware wallet (coldcard.com); "co-founder" on screen.
8. **Ou called it unrealistic because it was coordinated online first.** Same CoinDesk article, Ou: "It was a fun demo, but
   obviously unrealistic because we coordinated everything online before sending the radio signals".
9. **Recovery words restore a wallet anywhere, no company needed; store them physically.**
   Ledger: "even if your original wallet provider doesn't exist anymore, you can still enter your words into another compatible
   wallet to restore your access" / "Recording your seed phrase on something physical— be that a hardware wallet or a piece of
   paper—is a much better option." https://www.ledger.com/academy/crypto/whats-a-secret-recovery-phrase
   Two copies in two places: the account's standing step (landfill-story, bitcoin-only-money); paper burns (Ledger's own house-fire
   example), so two places.

Not used (couldn't verify well enough or not needed): goTenna/txTenna mesh relay (CoinDesk, blocked here; app status today
