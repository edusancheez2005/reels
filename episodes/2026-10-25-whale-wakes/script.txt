On October twelfth, one Bitcoin address moved four thousand, five hundred and eighty-two bitcoin. About three hundred and seventy-seven million dollars. The fee? Sixty cents. Those coins landed there in March twenty twenty, when they were worth forty million. Within hours, trackers had flagged it. Because every Bitcoin transfer is public. Before it's confirmed, it waits in the mempool, where bots read every single one. This one had one input, and five outputs. Four of a thousand bitcoin each, and the rest. Then everyone watched where it went next. An exchange would hint at selling. New wallets usually mean storage. Nearly two weeks later? Not one coin has moved. So when a whale wakes up, don't watch the price. Watch where the coins go.
