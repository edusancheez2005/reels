About three point seven million bitcoin are probably lost forever. That's nearly one in five, worth around three hundred billion dollars today. How? Bitcoin has no password reset. Owning it means holding a private key, a secret code that moves your coins. Lose the key, and the coins stay on the public record, visible to all, but nobody can move them. In twenty twenty-one, programmer Stefan Thomas had seven thousand and two bitcoin behind a password he'd forgotten. His drive allows ten guesses, then locks for good. He'd used eight. Even the very first fifty bitcoin, from the first block in two thousand and nine, can never be spent: a quirk in the code. Only twenty-one million will ever exist, so lost coins are never replaced. The real total is a guess: some may be savers who never sell. If you hold your own, write down your recovery words, the backup for your key.
