A hedge fund with two Nobel prize winners on its team lost 44% of its value in one month, August 1998.
Borrowed money turns a small mistake into the whole firm.
Long-Term Capital Management made small bets that the prices of similar bonds would move back together, and borrowed about $30 for every $1 of its own to make those bets pay. In August 1998 Russia stopped paying its debts, investors rushed to safety, and the gaps got wider instead. On 23 September 1998, fourteen banks and brokerage firms put in $3.6 billion to stop it collapsing. The Federal Reserve hosted the talks and lent none of its own money.
