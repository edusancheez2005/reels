## Facts (each with its source)
1. Two Nobel laureates: "Led by a team of market experts including two Nobel laureates, LTCM was a hedge fund well known for using sophisticated mathematical models to make impressive profits." (A). The 1997 economics prize went to Robert C. Merton and Myron S. Scholes "for a new method to determine the value of derivatives" (B, 14 Oct 1997); "the flashy Greenwich, Connecticut, firm that both Merton and Scholes had joined five years earlier" (D). The voice says "two Nobel prize winners" and names nobody.
2. Founded: "Founded by former Salomon Brothers Vice Chairman John Meriwether in February 1994" (A). Not named in the voice.
3. What the bets were: "LTCM exploited temporary price differences between similar types of securities."; "By the nature of its strategy, LTCM earned low returns on each dollar invested. In order to earn high rates of return on its capital, the fund borrowed considerable money to leverage its positions." (A). Script: "Two near-identical bonds, slightly different prices. They bet the gap would close." (A also lists government bonds first among its markets.)
4. The one number: "As of the end of 1997, LTCM was holding about $30 in debt for every $1 of capital" (A, citing Fortune). Script: "For every dollar of their own, thirty borrowed."
5. It had worked: returns of "43 percent in 1995, 41 percent in 1996" (A). Not in the voice.
6. The turn: "In August, Russia suddenly devalued its currency and stopped payments on its debt, spurring investors to seek safer and more liquid investments. LTCM had largely been betting on the spreads in its portfolios to converge, but in almost every case, they diverged" (A).
7. The hook: "The fund lost 44 percent of its value in August alone." (A). Script: "lost nearly half its value in one month". (D says 45 percent; C: the partners' letter acknowledged "52 percent losses on the year through August 31".)
8. The rescue: "On September 23, 1998, a group of fourteen banks and brokerage firms invested $3.6 billion in Long-Term Capital Management L.P. (LTCM) to prevent the firm's imminent collapse." (A); "fourteen firms put up $3.625 billion in capital in exchange for 90 percent of the fund's ownership" (A); E: "to buy control over the fund for $3.6 billion". C: "14 banks and securities firms agreed to participate". Script: "fourteen firms".
9. No public money: "While the Federal Reserve brought the market participants together and oversaw the refinancing, it did not put its own funds at risk." (A); "Not one penny of public money was spent or committed." (C). In the caption, not the voice.
10. Afterwards: LTCM "returned the last of the group's $3.6 billion investment by the end of 1999"; "LTCM's partners and other investors suffered substantial losses when their claim was reduced to 10 percent" (A). Caption only.
11. Arithmetic (mine, from fact 4): $30 borrowed + $1 own = $31 of holdings; a fall of 1/31 = 3.2% in what you hold equals all of your own dollar. Script: "Thirty to one: the bonds drop three percent, your dollar is gone." It is the sum, not a claim about what LTCM's holdings fell by.
12. The receipt on screen: source A's page, captured 25 Nov 2026 (headline, "September 1998", and the summary line "a group of 14 banks and brokerage firms invested $3.6 billion in LTCM to prevent the hedge fund's imminent collapse").
