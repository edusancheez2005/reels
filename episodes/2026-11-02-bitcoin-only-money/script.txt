If Bitcoin were the only money, the world would share one network handling about seven payments a second.
A thought experiment, not a prediction. Meet Maya, who lives there.
She buys a coffee. Visa can handle sixty-five thousand payments a second. Bitcoin, about seven. So her payment waits in a queue, and a bigger fee jumps it ahead. In April 2024, the average fee hit a hundred and twenty-eight dollars.
The milk's sour. No bank can cancel a Bitcoin payment, so only the café can refund her.
Her savings swing too: in 2022, bitcoin's price fell sixty-four percent.
Then she loses her recovery phrase, the twelve words that open her wallet. Nobody can reset them. Her savings are gone.
Supporters say nobody can print more bitcoin. Lightning, an add-on built on Bitcoin, makes small payments near-instant.
Two of Maya's problems are real today. Payments are final, so double-check before you send. Nobody can reset your phrase, so keep two offline copies.
