On Thursday, Anthropic, the maker of the chatbot Claude, raised eighty-one billion dollars in the biggest stock market debut ever.
That was its I.P.O., when a company sells pieces of itself, called shares, to investors for the first time.
Anthropic sold nine hundred and eighty million shares at eighty-three dollars each. The old record: the rocket company SpaceX, about seventy-five billion, in June.
Then the shares hit the Nasdaq. The first trade: a hundred and eleven fifty, over thirty percent above eighty-three.
By the end of the day: ninety-seven seventeen. Still above eighty-three, but anyone who paid that first price was down thirteen percent.
The day before, the crypto app Binance Wallet sold up to five hundred million dollars of tokens built to follow Anthropic's price.
But a token isn't a share. No vote, no cut of any profits paid out, just the price moves.
Two lessons. Check the price you'd actually pay: day one moved thirteen percent. And a token that tracks a stock isn't the stock. The vote and the payouts stay with the shares.
