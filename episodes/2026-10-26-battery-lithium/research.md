# How a lithium-ion battery stores energy — sources (checked 2026-10-26)

1. A lithium-ion cell has an anode (negative side) "commonly made out of graphite, the same material found in many pencils"; a cathode (positive side) "typically a combination of nickel, manganese, and cobalt oxides"; "a separator that prevents contact between the anode and cathode"; and "an electrolyte that moves lithium ions between the cathode and anode". In use, lithium ions move through the electrolyte from anode to cathode, converting "stored chemical energy" into electrical current; charging reverses the reactions. — Argonne National Laboratory, Science 101: Batteries: https://www.anl.gov/science-101/batteries
2. Electrodes are "layered materials that can store (intercalate) lithium ions in the gaps between their layers". In use, "electrons travel from the anode to the cathode through the external circuit" while "the positive lithium ions travel through the electrolyte from anode to cathode"; on recharge the electrons and ions flow back to the anode. — Chemistry World, 9 Oct 2019: https://www.chemistryworld.com/news/why-have-lithium-ion-batteries-just-won-the-chemistry-nobel-prize/4010479.article
3. Separator: "Although ions pass freely between the electrodes, the separator is an isolator with no electrical conductivity." Li-ion separators are polyolefin (polyethylene, polypropylene) — i.e. plastic film. — Battery University BU-306: https://batteryuniversity.com/article/bu-306-what-is-the-function-of-the-separator
4. Goodenough "demonstrated that cobalt oxide with intercalated lithium ions can produce as much as four volts"; Yoshino made the first commercially viable Li-ion battery in 1985; on market since 1991; "a lightweight, hardwearing battery that could be charged hundreds of times". — Nobel Prize press release, 2019 Chemistry: https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/chemistry/2019/press-release/
5. "John B. Goodenough, 97, just won the Nobel Prize in chemistry." "Goodenough is the oldest person to win a Nobel Prize." — CNN, 9 Oct 2019: https://edition.cnn.com/2019/10/09/us/john-goodenough-nobel-prize-trnd ; also Chemistry World (above): "the oldest ever winner of a Nobel prize".

## Simplifications (deliberate)
- "Doesn't store electricity, stores lithium on the wrong side": the battery stores chemical energy (ANL) — lithium sitting in the graphite is the charged state; it flows back to the oxide on discharge. Plain-language framing of fact 1–2.
- "Each atom drops an electron": on discharge lithium leaves the graphite as an ion (Li⁺) and its electron goes through the external circuit (fact 2).
- "Metal oxide" kept generic: cathode chemistries vary (nickel/manganese/cobalt oxides per ANL). "The man who found that oxide" = Goodenough's lithium cobalt oxide cathode (fact 4); the on-screen card names cobalt oxide and paraphrases "as much as four volts" as "up to four volts".
- NOT used: separator thickness vs a human hair (no single source compared them); exact cycle counts (vary by chemistry; "hundreds of times" per Nobel).
