There's a plastic wall inside your phone, thinner than most human hairs. If it fails, your phone can catch fire. Here's why it's there. Your battery has two sides. Graphite on one, a metal oxide on the other. Charging pushes lithium into the graphite. Unplug, and it wants to go back. But to cross, each lithium atom leaves an electron behind, and the wall only lets the atom through. So electrons take the long way, through your phone. That detour is your power. Now pierce the wall. The electrons take the shortcut, straight through. The cell starts heating itself, faster and faster, until it vents, smokes and burns. In twenty sixteen, a tiny metal burr pierced that wall inside some Samsung batteries. Samsung recalled every Galaxy Note Seven, about three million phones. All because of a wall thinner than most hairs.
