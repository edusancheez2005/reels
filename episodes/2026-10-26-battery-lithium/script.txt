Your phone battery doesn't store electricity. It stores lithium, parked on the wrong side. Inside are two layered materials. On one side, graphite, the same stuff as pencil lead. On the other, a metal oxide. Between them, a liquid, and a thin plastic wall. Plug in, and the charger drives lithium out of the oxide. It drifts through the liquid, slips through the wall, and slots between the graphite layers. That's a full battery. Unplug, and the lithium wants to go home. But to cross, each atom drops an electron, and the wall won't let electrons through. So they take the long way, through your phone. That flow is the power. Then the charger pushes it all back again, hundreds of times. And the man who found that oxide? He won the Nobel Prize at ninety-seven, the oldest winner ever.
