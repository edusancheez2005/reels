## Facts (each with its source)
1. The price: the hot dog and soda combo costs $1.50 and has not changed. B: "The price has remained the same since 1985." A (headline): "Costco hot dogs have cost $1.50 since the 1980s"; "since it first hit menus in the mid-1980s". D: Costco "recently celebrated the 40th anniversary of the offering" (said Sept 2025, so 1985). Script: "since 1985".
   Disagreement, noted: I and J say the hot dog cart began in 1984 (I: outside a San Diego warehouse; C says Portland, 1985). The voice says only "since 1985", the year Costco's own anniversary and CNN use, and says nothing about where.
2. What it is: a quarter-pound all-beef hot dog and a 20 oz soda with refill (A, and the menu board in A's photo: "1/4 POUND PLUS ALL BEEF HOT DOG AND 20 oz. SODA (With Refill) 1.50"). Script: "hot dog and drink".
3. The executive and the founder: Craig Jelinek (later Costco's CEO) told it in a 2018 speech (G; I cites 425 Business, 18 April 2018): "I said, 'Jim, we can't sell this hot dog for a buck fifty. We are losing our rear ends.' He said, 'If you raise the effing hot dog, I will kill you. Figure it out.'" Jim Sinegal is Costco's co-founder. A: "according to legend, as well as Jelinek himself". B prints the same words "according to former CEO Craig Jelinek".
   Script: "An executive tells the co-founder: we're losing money. Raise it. The co-founder's answer, as that executive later told it: I will kill you. / Figure it out." Attributed to the executive's telling, nobody named in the voice; both halves are his words as Jelinek told them, with the opening clause and its swear word left out (plan: "no swearing" in the quote). The full quote goes in the sources comment.
4. What they did: G: "Instead of changing the price, Costco simply built its own hot dog manufacturing plant in Los Angeles."; Jelinek: "Now we are doing so much hot dog business that we've opened up another plant in Chicago". A: Costco began its own hot dog factories in 2008 "to cut down on supply chain costs". Before that the hot dogs were Hebrew National (I, J). Script: "So Costco built its own hot dog factory." (No year in the voice: the sources give 2008/2009 and don't date the conversation.)
5. How many: D, CFO Gary Millerchip on the 25 Sept 2025 earnings call: "in fiscal year 2025, we sold over 245 million hot dog combos" (fiscal year ended 31 Aug 2025). C: "In the fiscal year 2025, Costco set a new record, selling more than 245 million hot dog combos." Script: "In one year, Costco sold over two hundred and forty-five million of these." (not "last year": fiscal 2025 ended in Aug 2025) ("Mostly to your friend." is the joke.)
6. Inflation since 1985 (the one sum): CPI-U, all items, not seasonally adjusted: 1985 annual average 107.6 (F); October 2026 "an index level of 337.511 (1982-84=100)" (E; F has the same). 337.511 / 107.6 = 3.137, so prices are about 3.1 times 1985's ("roughly tripled"), and $1.50 × 3.137 = $4.71 ("about four seventy"). Cross-checks: C (March 2026): "that $1.50 deal would set you back $4.65 today"; B (May 2024): "three times as expensive today - nearly $4.50"; A (June 2024): "closer to $4.40".
7. Staying: C, CEO Ron Vachris, March 2026: "The hot dog price will not change as long as I'm around." B, CFO Gary Millerchip, earnings call of 30 May 2024: "To clear up some recent media speculation, I also want to confirm the $1.50 hot dog price is safe." H, then-CFO Richard Galanti, Sept 2022: "hold the price on the hot dog and the soda a little longer — forever." Script: "Costco's boss says it's staying." This reports what the company says; it is not a forecast of ours.
8. Why a shop would do it (caption only, not the voice): A calls it loss-leader pricing; C: membership fees were about 64% of profits in fiscal 2025.
9. Seasons: 1985 is season one, so 1999 is season fifteen and 2026 is season forty-two. Corded phones in 1985, flip phones by 1999 and smartphones now are set dressing, not claims.
10. The receipt on screen: source A's page captured 26 Nov 2026 (headline, date, and the photo of the food-court menu board showing 1.50).
