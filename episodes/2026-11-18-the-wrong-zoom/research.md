## Facts used (every on-screen and spoken claim)

1. **The SEC suspended trading in Zoom Technologies, Inc. (ticker ZOOM) from 26 March to 8 April 2020.**
   - SEC order, Release No. 34-88477, dated 25 March 2020: trading suspended "at 9:30 a.m. EDT on March 26, 2020, and terminating at 11:59 p.m. EDT on April 8, 2020." https://www.sec.gov/files/litigation/suspensions/2020/34-88477.pdf (index page: https://www.sec.gov/file/34-88477pdf)
   - Bloomberg, 26 Mar 2020: trading "was suspended by the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission Thursday through April 8." https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2020-03-26/zoom-technologies-trading-suspended-amid-ticker-confusion
   → Script: "The regulator froze it for two weeks, worried people were mixing them up. Nobody could sell." (26 Mar to 8 Apr = 14 days. A trading suspension under Exchange Act s.12(k) stops trading in the security, so holders could neither sell nor buy for those two weeks.)
2. **Why (the order's own words):** concerns about "investors confusing this issuer with a similarly-named NASDAQ-listed issuer", and about the adequacy of public information "in light of the absence of any public disclosure by the company since 2015." (SEC order, above.)
   → Script: "Silent since 2015." / "worried people were mixing them up."
3. **Where it was:** "ZOOM is a Delaware corporation that reported in 2014 having its principal executive offices in Beijing, China." (SEC order.) Fortune: "The company most recently reported that it was headquartered in Beijing."
   → Not spoken; the caption says "last reported an office in Beijing in 2014". (The draft's "phone-parts company" is cut: only one outlet, TNW, says "reportedly".)
4. **The two tickers:** Zoom Technologies = ZOOM (quoted over the counter); Zoom Video Communications = ZM (Nasdaq). SEC tweet quoted by The Next Web: "Zoom Technologies (ticker ZOOM) is NOT the similarly-named popular video communications company." Fortune, 26 Mar 2020, headline: "'ZOOM' stock halted after investors confuse it with Zoom Video stock". https://fortune.com/2020/03/26/zoom-stock-halt-zm-ticker/ · https://thenextweb.com/news/sec-halts-zoom-after-coronavirus-traders-confuse-it-for-zoom-app
   → Script: "The video company's stock code is Z, M. You own Zoom Technologies."
5. **The rise: about $3 to over $20 in a month.** Fortune: "the 'ZOOM' stock had increased nearly sevenfold—from around $3 a share to more than $20—in the span of a month." The Next Web: "from around $3 in mid-February to $20.90 last Friday" (20 Mar 2020). (The draft's "about nine hundred percent that year" could not be sourced and is cut. Markets Insider says "more than 800% year-to-date"; not used.)
   → Script: "It's already gone from about three dollars to over twenty in a month." ("You" buy after the rise, in late March; the rise ran mid-February to 20 March.)
6. **The wrong Zoom was outrunning the real one.** Bloomberg, 26 Mar 2020: "Shares of the now-halted Zoom Technologies more than tripled over the past five weeks, while Zoom Video rose more than 30% during the same period." Fortune: Zoom Video "has surged 50% since the broader market's peak last month"; Zoom Technologies "had risen more than 240% over the same time frame".
   → Script: "By then, the wrong … Zoom was rising faster than the real one." (a comparison of the two rises over the same weeks; no cause claimed)
7. **It had happened before, in 2019.** CNBC, 18 Apr 2019 ("Investors appear to trade wrong Zoom company shares before IPO"): "within two hours of trading, shares of ZOOM surged more than 80%"; "Zoom Technologies (ticker ZOOM) is not the company Zoom Video Communications (ticker ZM)". https://www.cnbc.com/2019/04/18/investors-appear-to-trade-wrong-zoom-company-shares-before-ipo.html
   AP, 18 Apr 2019 ("2 Zooms soared after the IPO: Did you buy the wrong one?"): Zoom Technologies "surged as much as 124.5% in Thursday morning trading." https://www.ksl.com/article/46534511
   → Script: "And in 2019. Same four letters."
8. **March 2020 context:** lockdowns; Zoom Video's share price rose "from below $80 in January to an all-time-high of $159.56 on March 19" (The Next Web). Not said as a number.
