Forty billion dollars, gone in a week, when a coin built to always be worth one dollar broke.
It was a stablecoin called UST, from Terraform Labs, co-founded by Do Kwon.
A rival, USDC, holds a dollar per coin. UST used a second coin instead: Luna.
One UST could always swap for a dollar's worth of new Luna. If UST dipped, traders bought it cheap, swapped it, and UST climbed back.
In 2021, UST slipped. Kwon said the swap fixed it. Really, he'd had a trading firm secretly buy UST.
A savings app, Anchor, paid up to nineteen and a half percent on UST, and fourteen billion piled in.
In May 2022, five billion fled Anchor. UST fell again, too big to secretly prop up. Holders swapped it for new Luna, flooding the market, and Luna crashed from eighty-four dollars to under a tenth of a cent.
Kwon got fifteen years for fraud.
Holders made two mistakes: trusting a coin-backed dollar, and reading a huge yield as safety. When the backing is a coin, panic sinks both. Check what backs a stablecoin, and who pays the yield.
