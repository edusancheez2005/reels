Eight hundred and fifty thousand bitcoin, gone. In 2014, the world's biggest bitcoin exchange said it had lost them.
Mt. Gox was a website in Tokyo where people kept their bitcoin.
French programmer Mark Karpelès bought it in 2011. That same year, US prosecutors say, hackers got into its server.
Over two years, the hackers took about six hundred and forty-seven thousand coins.
Customers only saw a number on the website, so they couldn't see their coins vanish.
In February 2014, Mt. Gox froze withdrawals and filed for bankruptcy.
In 2019, Karpelès was cleared of pocketing customers' money, but convicted of tampering with records.
Repayments started in 2024, ten years later, through a court-appointed trustee.
About thirty-four thousand coins still haven't been paid out.
The customers' mistake: every coin sat with one company, and they couldn't check.
