## Facts (each used on screen or in the voice)

1. **Mt. Gox filed for bankruptcy protection saying 850,000 bitcoins had disappeared**: about 750,000 customers' coins and
   about 100,000 of its own. It suspended trading on 25 Feb 2014 and filed in Tokyo District Court on 28 Feb 2014.
   Computerworld (IDG News), 21 Mar 2014: https://www.computerworld.com/article/1390713/mt-gox-finds-116m-worth-of-bitcoins.html
   (screenshot: assets/cw.png). Also Decrypt, 20 Oct 2026 ("roughly 850,000 BTC went missing"), The Block 2025 ("lost 850,000 BTC").
2. **It was the biggest bitcoin exchange.** DOJ (9 Jun 2023): "At the time, Mt. Gox was the largest Bitcoin exchange in existence,
   servicing thousands of users worldwide." https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/russian-nationals-charged-hacking-one-cryptocurrency-exchange-and-illicitly-operating-another
   (AFP via FMT says it "at one point claimed to be handling around 80 percent of all global bitcoin transactions"; Wikipedia says
   "over 70%". Not used: the share figures differ and are the company's own claim.)
3. **Based in Tokyo** (Shibuya). DOJ: wallets and keys stored "on a computer server in Japan"; Computerworld: "The Tokyo exchange".
   Wikipedia: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mt._Gox
4. **Mark Karpelès, a French developer living in Japan, bought the site from founder Jed McCaleb in March 2011.** Wikipedia (above);
   AFP via FMT calls him "the 33-year-old Frenchman" (2019) and "the former creator of the MtGox bitcoin exchange".
   Script says "French programmer Mark Karpelès bought it in 2011".
5. **The theft began in 2011.** DOJ: "in or about September 2011, Bilyuchenko, Verner, and their co-conspirators allegedly gained
   unauthorized access to the server holding the cryptocurrency wallets for Mt. Gox ... From September 2011 through at least May 2014
   ... allegedly caused the theft of at least approximately 647,000 bitcoins from Mt. Gox, representing the vast majority of the
   bitcoins belonging to Mt. Gox's customers." Same page, headline paragraph: "charged with conspiring to launder approximately
   647,000 bitcoins from their hack of Mt. Gox" (screenshot: assets/doj.png). Charges, not convictions: the script says
   "US prosecutors say". "over two years" = Sep 2011 to at least Feb/May 2014.
6. **Customers couldn't tell.** Mt. Gox stored customers' wallets and keys itself (DOJ: "Mt. Gox stored the cryptocurrency wallets
   containing its customers' bitcoin, and the corresponding private keys ... on a computer server in Japan"); the theft was only
   "revealed" in 2014 (DOJ: "Mt. Gox ceased operations in 2014 after the theft was revealed"); WizSec (2015, via Wikipedia): coins
   "stolen straight out of the Mt. Gox hot wallet over time, beginning in late 2011". The line "customers only saw a number on the
   website, so they couldn't tell" is the plain-words version of this (an exchange account shows a balance, not coins you hold).
7. **Froze withdrawals, then bankruptcy, Feb 2014.** Bitcoin withdrawals halted 7 Feb 2014; trading suspended 24/25 Feb; Tokyo filing
   28 Feb 2014. Wikipedia (above); Computerworld (filing date, trading suspension).
8. (Cut from the voice for length.) **Found 200,000 bitcoins in an old wallet.** Computerworld: "the failed exchange said it found 200,000 bitcoins in an old-format
   digital wallet, reducing the number of bitcoins missing to 650,000"; "an old format wallet used before June 2011". Statement dated
   20 Mar 2014, three weeks after the 28 Feb filing ("Weeks later").
9. **Karpelès: guilty of manipulating data, not guilty of embezzlement; suspended sentence (15 Mar 2019, Tokyo).** AFP via Free
   Malaysia Today: "Mark Karpeles was found not guilty of embezzlement but guilty of manipulation of computer data at the MtGox

## The mechanism and the surprise
- Mechanism: customers' coins and the keys that move them sat on Mt. Gox's own server. Customers saw only a balance on a website.
  Thieves drained the wallets slowly from 2011, and nobody outside could check, until withdrawals froze in 2014.
- Surprise: the theft started the same year Karpelès took over, and it's still not fully paid back in 2026.

## Lesson (the story's own mistake, and the fix)
- Mistake: everyone's coins sat with one company, and nobody could check them.
- Step for today: send a small amount from your exchange to a wallet only you control, so you know you can get out (a balance on an
  exchange is a promise). Different from the FTX reel's line (8 Nov: "keep only what you trade on an exchange").
