Onions are the only crop Americans are banned from trading futures on. Blame two traders and 30 million pounds of onions.
In late 1955, Vincent Kosuga and Sam Siegel held practically all the onions in Chicago available to the exchange, about 1,000 rail carloads. Growers bought 287 carloads from them on a promise that the rest stayed off the exchange. The pair then bet on the price falling, and by 15 March 1956 the March contract hit ten cents for a 50-pound bag. The story goes the empty bag cost more. In 1960 the US Department of Agriculture ruled they had manipulated the price. Congress had already banned onion futures, on 28 August 1958, and the ban still stands.
