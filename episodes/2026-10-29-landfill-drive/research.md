# The hard drive in a Welsh landfill — sources & verification (checked 29 Oct 2026)

Hook: "In 2013, a man in Wales threw away a hard drive. The bitcoin on it is worth over $600 million today."

## The drive and the coins
- James Howells (Newport, Wales) mined bitcoin from 2009; a hard drive holding the private key was thrown out in 2013
  (between 20 June and 10 August 2013) and ended up at the Docksway landfill, Newport. — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bitcoin_buried_in_Newport_landfill
  (cites The Guardian 2013, BBC News 28 Nov 2013); thrown out in 2013 while clearing his office — ITV, 14 Jan 2021:
  https://itv.com/news/wales/2021-01-14/man-offers-council-millions-to-find-hard-drive-with-230-million-of-bitcoin-stored-on-after-it-was-thrown-out-by-mistake [Certain as reported]

## The offer and the refusal
- Jan 2021: Howells offered Newport City Council a 25% share ("a quarter") if it dug up the drive; the council: "we cannot
  assist him in this matter." — ITV, 14 Jan 2021 (URL above) [Certain]
- Council reasons over the years: cost, environmental permit, safety (Wikipedia, citing Wired 2017 and The Guardian 2021).

## The court
- Howells v Newport City Council [2025] EWHC 22 (Ch), High Court (Cardiff), 9 Jan 2025, Judge Keyser KC: claim struck out,
  "no realistic prospect of succeeding". Under s.14(6)(c) Control of Pollution Act 1974, items delivered to the landfill
  "shall belong" to the authority: once deposited, the drive became the council's property.
  — https://11kbw.com/knowledge-events/case/council-not-liable-for-value-of-bitcoin ; The Block (URL above) [Certain]
  Script: "a judge ruled the drive became the council's once it was dumped there."
- Court of Appeal refused permission to appeal, 13 Mar 2025 (Nugee LJ). — https://protos.com/bitcoin-landfill-guy-loses-uk-appeal-plans-human-rights-case
  (used on screen only as "Appeal refused · Mar 2025" if at all)
