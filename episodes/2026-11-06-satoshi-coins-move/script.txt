About one point one million bitcoin, believed to be Satoshi's, have never moved. What if they moved? A thought experiment.
Satoshi Nakamoto, Bitcoin's unknown creator, went quiet in 2011. That number is researcher Sergio Lerner's 2013 estimate.
If those coins moved, bots watching Bitcoin's public record would sound alarms. In May 2020, fifty coins from February 2009 moved. A bot guessed they might be Satoshi's, and bitcoin fell over seven percent that day.
But a move only proves someone has the secret code that spends those coins. That code can also sign a message, without moving anything.
Take Craig Wright, an Australian computer scientist who said he was Satoshi. In court, he listed early addresses, like account numbers, as his. Then someone with the codes for a hundred and forty-five of those addresses signed: Craig Steven Wright is a liar and a fraud. In 2024, a UK judge ruled he isn't Satoshi.
The 2020 sellers took a bot's guess as proof. A move doesn't prove who. So if a headline says Satoshi is selling, look for a signature.
