In 1720 Isaac Newton sold his South Sea stock at a big profit. Two months later he bought back in, near the top of the bubble.
He sold on 19 and 23 April 1720. In mid-June he moved almost all his money back in. The price peaked at £1,050 and was £124 by December. One study (Andrew Odlyzko, 2019) puts his loss at about £10,000, a third of his fortune; the famous version says £20,000. In 1724 he wrote: "I lost very much by the South Sea company which makes my pockets empty." He still died a wealthy man.
