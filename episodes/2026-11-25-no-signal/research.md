## Facts (all checked 25 Nov 2026, 04:00–04:40 London)

1. **The real case, date and time.** "shortly after 4:00 pm ET on Tuesday, January 9, 2024, an unauthorized party gained access" to @SECGov; it "made one post at 4:11 pm ET purporting to announce the Commission's approval of spot bitcoin exchange-traded funds." — SEC statement page, https://www.sec.gov/secgov-x-account
2. **It was a SIM swap of the number behind the account.** "the unauthorized party obtained control of the SEC cell phone number associated with the account in an apparent "SIM swap" attack"; "Access to the phone number occurred via the telecom carrier, not via SEC systems." — https://www.sec.gov/secgov-x-account (Jan. 22, 2024 statement)
3. **The second login check was off.** "While multi-factor authentication (MFA) had previously been enabled on the @SECGov X account, it was disabled by X Support, at the staff's request, in July 2023 due to issues accessing the account." MFA "remained disabled until staff reenabled it after the account was compromised on January 9." — https://www.sec.gov/secgov-x-account → script: "Its second login check was off."
4. **The homemade ID and the phone shop.** Eric Council Jr. "used an identification card printer to create a fraudulent identification card" and "used the identification card to impersonate the victim and gain access to the victim's phone number for the purpose of accessing the SEC's X account." Sentenced 16 May 2025 to 14 months. — DOJ, https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/alabama-man-sentenced-14-months-connection-securities-and-exchange-commission-x-hack-spiked ; DC US Attorney release "Alabama Man Sentenced in Hack of SEC X Account that Spiked the Value of Bitcoin": "Council used his portable ID card printer to create a physical ID", used at an AT&T store in Huntsville, Alabama; "Agents recovered a fake identification card and a portable ID card printer." — https://www.justice.gov/usao-dc/pr/alabama-man-sentenced-hack-sec-x-account-spiked-value-bitcoin
   The Register: he "told the worker that he was an FBI employee who had broken his phone and needed a new SIM card"; put the SIM in a new phone "to receive two-factor security reset codes"; "shared the reset code with his co-conspirator, who used it to access the SEC's X account". — https://www.theregister.com/2025/05/19/sim_swapper_sec_x_account/

5. **Fake bitcoin news moved the price.** "the price of BTC increased by more than $1,000 per BTC" then "decreased by more than $2,000 per BTC" (DOJ, URL in 4). CNBC: "bitcoin prices initially shooting up to nearly $48,000 from a low that day of just above $45,000" then "prices fell below $46,000." — https://www.cnbc.com/2024/01/22/new-details-emerge-about-secs-x-account-hack-including-sim-swap.html (the voice says only "Fake bitcoin news went out.")
6. **What a SIM swap is.** FBI: "SIM swapping is a malicious technique where criminal actors target mobile carriers to gain access to victims' bank accounts." 2021: "IC3 received 1,611 SIM swapping complaints with adjusted losses of more than $68 million" (2018–2020: 320 complaints, about $12 million). — FBI IC3 PSA I-020822-PSA, https://www.ic3.gov/PSA/2022/PSA220208 (caption only)
7. **Rule one is the FBI's own first tip.** "Do not advertise information about financial assets" (incl. crypto ownership on social media). — https://www.ic3.gov/PSA/2022/PSA220208
8. **Rule two is the FBI's tip.** "Be aware of any changes in SMS-based connectivity." (losing signal for no reason = a sign the number moved). — same PSA
9. **The step, part 1: carriers lock numbers.** AT&T Wireless Account Lock (1 Jul 2025) "prevents anyone from ... conducting a SIM swap" and blocks "wireless number transfers" — https://about.att.com/story/2025/wireless-account-lock.html ; Verizon "SIM Protection and Number Lock... prevent SIM swaps and phone number transfers"; T-Mobile settings to "prevent SIM swaps" and "block unauthorized number port outs" — TechCrunch, 9 Jul 2025, https://techcrunch.com/2025/07/09/how-to-protect-your-cell-phone-number-from-sim-swap-attacks/
10. **The step, part 2: authenticator apps.** FBI: "Use strong multi-factor authentication methods such as biometrics, physical security tokens, or standalone authentication applications" — PSA above. AT&T: "Authenticator app on your smartphone: generates time-sensitive codes" — AT&T URL above.
11. **The FCC made carriers tighten SIM changes.** Report and Order "Protecting Consumers from SIM-Swap and Port-Out Fraud", Federal Register 8 Dec 2023 — https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2023/12/08/2023-26338/protecting-consumers-from-sim-swap-and-port-out-fraud (background, not in the voice)
