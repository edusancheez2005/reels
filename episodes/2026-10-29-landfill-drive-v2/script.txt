In twenty thirteen, a man in Wales threw away a hard drive. The bitcoin it unlocks is worth over six hundred million dollars today. He's James Howells, a computer engineer from Newport. In two thousand and nine, his laptop mined about eight thousand bitcoin, he says, by helping run the network. He spilled a drink on it, sold the parts, and kept the drive in a drawer. Clearing out his office, he binned the wrong drive. Months later, he checked his backups. Nothing. His key, the code that moves the coins, was in the landfill. He offered the council that runs it a quarter to dig. They refused. He sued. In twenty twenty-five a judge threw it out: once dumped, the drive was the council's. Two mistakes: one copy of his key, on a drive he treated like junk. A lost key can't be reset. Write down your recovery words, the backup for your key, and keep two copies in two places. Label any drive that holds a key.
